User holds work to a "perfectly smooth" bar, not just "works." Mentioned
(2026-06-20) that before working with LLMs they never called something done
until it worked that well — contrasted with a corporate experience where
work was later undermined by last-minute "cost savings" (swapping
agreed-upon hardware for discount-shop substitutes days before shipping to
a customer), told with dry humor, not as a request for action.

**Why this matters:** once a root cause is diagnosed and a fix lands, the
user will often keep tuning cosmetic/secondary details (e.g. exact
`fade_start_geom` pixel values, animation feel) far past the point where
the underlying bug is fixed. This isn't scope creep to redirect — it's
their normal craft process. Don't suggest "good enough, let's stop" once
the core problem is solved; let them keep iterating, and stay available
rather than pushing toward closure.

**How to apply:** distinguish "the bug is fixed" from "the user is done" —
these are different milestones for them. When they say they'll continue
alone, take that at face value (not as a sign something went wrong) and
keep the door open rather than summarizing toward a close.
