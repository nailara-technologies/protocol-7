Described first-hand: `bin/atom-delta-term` (years-old tool, decodes
offset/non-printable checksum bytes as ANSI color/control codes,
rendered live to the terminal — confirmed via source: `Digest::BMW`/
`Digest::Elf` entropy through `Term::ANSIColor`) produced a genuinely
disorienting after-effect on first exposure: temporary inability to read
terminal text at all — not just difficulty, but losing the ability to
even *detect* that something was text, described as a "digital blur"
that dissolved meaning, "washed off" the editor window, lasting a while
after exposure ended.

**Important context, user-supplied**: `atom-delta-term` is an oracle
tool — same family as the much simpler `bin/question` (takes a question
as argv, feeds it through the harmonic truth-checker, returns TRUE/
FALSE) — just rendering the checksum entropy as a dense ANSI visual
field to be *read* for meaning, rather than a plain answer. User's own
correction: better framed as *anti-entropic* than "entropic" — the whole
harmonic-truth apparatus (division-by-13, rotation-invariant truth-
family) is about extracting deterministic order/structure from data
built to look maximally random (cryptographic checksum bytes), not
embracing randomness. The disorienting exposure happened during genuine,
sustained, attentive engagement [ actively trying to detect structure in
data designed to resist it ], not passive/incidental exposure to noise —
a much more demanding act than glancing at a glitchy terminal, and
plausibly part of why the effect was strong enough to linger.

**Why this matters beyond one old tool**: this is the same category of
concern as [[feedback-user-screen-brightness-sensitivity]] (CRITICAL) —
a real physical/perceptual reaction, not aesthetic preference — but a
different trigger: rapid, high-entropy, densely-cycling visual patterns,
not steady brightness level. The user's own framing of that memory
("nervous system... recovering from the impact") applies here too.

**Directly relevant to [[vision-orbital-hop-sequence-hyperspace-flight-animation]]**:
that note enthusiastically chases a "psychedelic" effect as a deliberately
desirable property — fast-paging cubic-space frames at arbitrary scale/
position, explicitly citing the disorienting-in-a-good-way sensation as
the discovery worth building on. This memory doesn't mean don't build
that — it means: if it ever gets implemented, playback speed / cycling
rate needs to be a real, tunable, defaulted-conservative parameter, not
an afterthought, and should probably default to something well short of
"as fast/dense as technically possible." Test cautiously, watch for the
same reported effect, don't ramp intensity on the assumption that faster/
denser is simply "more of the good thing" — this user has direct, lived
evidence that isn't always true.

**How to apply**: for any future rapid-cycling, strobe-like, or dense
high-entropy visual rendering work for this user (hop-sequence animation,
living icons, any ANSI/terminal color-cycling revival, etc.) — default to
a conservative cycling rate, make speed adjustable and easy to slow down
or stop, and treat "it felt psychedelic" as a signal to check in about
comfort, not just a success metric.
